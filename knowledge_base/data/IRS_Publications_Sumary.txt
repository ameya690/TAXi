### 1. IRS Publication 17: Your Federal Income Tax (For Individuals)

**Purpose and Audience:**
This is the IRS's comprehensive annual guide for individual taxpayers. Think of it as the master instruction manual for preparing a standard U.S. federal income tax return (Form 1040). Its audience is essentially every individual taxpayer, from those with simple W-2 income to those with more complex situations involving investments or small business income.

**Key Topics Covered:**
Publication 17 is organized to walk you through the entire process of filing a tax return, generally in the same order as the Form 1040 itself. Key sections include:

* **Filing Requirements:** Who must file a tax return, what the filing deadlines are, and which filing status to use (e.g., Single, Married Filing Jointly, Head of Household).
* **Dependents:** The detailed rules and tests (e.g., relationship, age, residency) for determining if you can claim someone as a qualifying child or qualifying relative.
* **Income:** A thorough breakdown of what the IRS considers taxable income. This covers everything from wages, salaries, and tips to interest, dividends, retirement distributions, gig economy income, and capital gains.
* **Adjustments to Income ("Above-the-Line" Deductions):** These are deductions you can take even if you don't itemize. The publication details common adjustments like deductions for IRA contributions, student loan interest, and health savings accounts (HSAs). The result of this calculation is your Adjusted Gross Income (AGI), a critical number in tax calculations.
* **Deductions ("Below-the-Line" Deductions):** It explains the difference between taking the Standard Deduction versus itemizing. If you itemize, it provides the rules for deducting expenses like state and local taxes (SALT), mortgage interest, charitable contributions, and medical expenses.
* **Tax Credits:** It covers the most common tax credits, which are more valuable than deductions because they reduce your tax dollar-for-dollar. This includes the Child Tax Credit, education credits, and credits for energy efficiency.
* **Calculating Your Tax:** How to use the tax tables or tax computation worksheets to find your final tax liability before payments and withholding are applied.

**Summary:**
Publication 17 is the foundational document for understanding your individual tax obligations. It provides the official definitions, rules, and exceptions for nearly every line item on the Form 1040. While too dense to read cover-to-cover, it is the ultimate reference guide to consult when you have a question about a specific part of your tax return.

### 2. IRS Publication 596: Earned Income Credit (EIC)

**Purpose and Audience:**
This is a highly specialized guide focused on a single, powerful, and complex tax credit: the Earned Income Credit (EIC or EITC). Its audience is low-to-moderate-income working individuals and families. The publication is designed as a procedural manual to help taxpayers determine if they are eligible for the credit and, if so, how to calculate it.

**Key Topics Covered:**
The entire publication is structured as a series of tests and rules to establish eligibility.

* **Rules for Everyone:** It starts with three universal rules: you must have a valid Social Security Number, your filing status cannot be "Married Filing Separately," and your investment income must be below a certain threshold.
* **Rules for a Qualifying Child:** This is the most critical section. It lays out the four specific tests that a child must meet to be your "qualifying child" for the EIC:
    1.  **Relationship:** The child must be your son, daughter, stepchild, foster child, sibling, etc.
    2.  **Age:** The child must be under 19, under 24 if a full-time student, or any age if permanently and totally disabled.
    3.  **Residency:** The child must have lived with you in the U.S. for more than half the year.
    4.  **Joint Return:** The child cannot have filed a joint return with their spouse.
* **Rules Without a Qualifying Child:** It details the separate, stricter eligibility requirements for taxpayers who do not have a qualifying child. This includes age requirements (you must be between 25 and 64) and residency rules.
* **Earned Income:** It defines what counts as "earned income" (wages, self-employment profit) and shows the income thresholds for the credit, which phase in and then phase out as income rises.
* **Calculating the Credit:** It provides the official EITC worksheets and tables that you use to find the exact amount of your credit based on your income, filing status, and number of qualifying children (from zero to three).

**Summary:**
Publication 596 is a focused, step-by-step workbook. It uses flowcharts, checklists, and examples to guide users through the intricate eligibility requirements for the EIC. Its primary purpose is to prevent improper claims while ensuring that all who are truly eligible can successfully claim this valuable refundable credit.

### 3. IRS Publication 463: Travel, Gift, and Car Expenses

**Purpose and Audience:**
This publication is for business owners, self-employed individuals (freelancers, contractors), and employees who incur business-related expenses. It provides the specific rules for deducting the costs of business travel, meals, entertainment, gifts, and vehicle usage.

**Key Topics Covered:**
The document is organized by the type of expense and heavily emphasizes the rules for substantiation.

* **Travel Expenses:** It defines what qualifies as "travel away from home" for business purposes. It details what is deductible, such as the cost of transportation (flights, trains), lodging, and a portion of meals.
* **Meals and Entertainment:** It explains the 50% limit on the deductibility of business meals and clarifies that entertainment expenses are generally no longer deductible.
* **Business Gifts:** It details the strict rule limiting the deduction for business gifts to $25 per recipient per year.
* **Car Expenses:** This is a major section that explains the two methods for calculating vehicle deductions:
    1.  **Standard Mileage Rate:** A simplified method where you multiply your business miles by a standard rate set annually by the IRS.
    2.  **Actual Expense Method:** A more complex method where you track and deduct the actual costs of operating your vehicle for business, including gas, oil, repairs, insurance, and depreciation.
* **Recordkeeping:** This is the most crucial theme of the entire publication. It repeatedly stresses the need for detailed, accurate, and contemporaneous records (e.g., receipts, mileage logs) to prove the amount, time, place, and business purpose of every expense.

**Summary:**
Publication 463 is a guide to compliance and substantiation. Its main lesson is that you cannot deduct business expenses without meticulous proof. It provides the specific rules and limitations for common business deductions and serves as a blueprint for the kind of records the IRS expects a taxpayer to maintain in case of an audit.
