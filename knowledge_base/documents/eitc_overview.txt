Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) Overview

The Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) is a benefit for working people with low to moderate income. To qualify, you must meet certain requirements and file a tax return, even if you do not owe any tax or are not required to file.

What is the EITC?
The EITC is a refundable tax credit that can reduce the amount of tax you owe and may result in a refund even if you don't owe any tax. The credit amount depends on your income, filing status, and number of qualifying children.

Basic Requirements:
- You must have earned income from employment or self-employment
- Your investment income must be $11,000 or less (for tax year 2024)
- You must have a valid Social Security number
- You must be a U.S. citizen or resident alien for the entire tax year
- You cannot file Form 2555 (Foreign Earned Income)
- Your filing status cannot be "married filing separately"

Income Limits:
For tax year 2024, the income limits are:
- No qualifying children: $18,591
- One qualifying child: $49,084
- Two qualifying children: $55,768
- Three or more qualifying children: $59,899

Qualifying Children:
A child must meet all of these tests to be a qualifying child:
- Relationship: Your son, daughter, stepchild, foster child, brother, sister, stepbrother, stepsister, or a descendant of any of them
- Age: Under age 19 at the end of the year (or under age 24 if a full-time student), or any age if permanently and totally disabled
- Residency: Must live with you in the United States for more than half the year
- Joint Return: Cannot file a joint return with their spouse (except to claim a refund)

How to Claim the EITC:
1. File your federal tax return
2. Complete Schedule EIC (Earned Income Credit) if you have a qualifying child
3. File Form 8867 if required by your tax professional

The IRS recommends using the EITC Assistant tool on IRS.gov to check your eligibility and estimate your credit amount.

Remember: This information is for general guidance only. Tax laws are complex and change frequently. Consult IRS Publication 596 or a qualified tax professional for your specific situation.

Source: IRS Publication 596 (2024)
