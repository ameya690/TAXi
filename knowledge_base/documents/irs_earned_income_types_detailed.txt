IRS Official: Types of Earned Income for EITC - Complete Guide
Source: IRS.gov Official Website

WHAT IS EARNED INCOME?

Earned income is income you receive from working. For the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC), earned income includes only certain types of compensation for work.

TYPES OF EARNED INCOME THAT COUNT FOR EITC

1. WAGES, SALARIES, AND TIPS

**What Counts:**
- Wages and salaries from Form W-2
- Tips you reported to your employer
- Tips you didn't report to your employer (still must be reported on your tax return)
- Elective salary deferrals (401k, 403b, etc.)
- Bonuses and commissions
- Overtime pay
- Hazard pay
- Severance pay (in some cases)

**Where to Find It:**
- Box 1 of Form W-2
- Box 7 of Form W-2 (Social Security tips)
- Additional unreported tips you add to your return

2. SELF-EMPLOYMENT INCOME

**What Counts:**
- Net profit from a business you own (Schedule C)
- Net profit from farming (Schedule F)
- Earnings from freelance or gig work
- Income as an independent contractor
- Earnings from 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC work

**How It's Calculated:**
Gross receipts MINUS business expenses = Net profit

**Important Notes:**
- Must subtract 1/2 of self-employment tax from net profit
- Loss from self-employment reduces earned income
- Cannot create negative earned income below zero

**Example:**
Gross business income: $45,000
Business expenses: $10,000
Net profit: $35,000
Self-employment tax: $4,945
Half of SE tax: $2,473
**Earned income for EITC: $32,527**

3. UNION STRIKE BENEFITS

**What Counts:**
- Benefits you receive from union strike funds
- Amounts paid by union during labor disputes

**Where to Find It:**
- May be reported on Form 1099-MISC
- Report as earned income even if not on a form

4. LONG-TERM DISABILITY BENEFITS

**What Counts (Before Minimum Retirement Age):**
- Long-term disability payments received before you reach minimum retirement age
- Payments from insurance policy paid for by employer

**What Doesn't Count (After Minimum Retirement Age):**
- Disability payments after minimum retirement age
- These become retirement income, not earned income

**Minimum Retirement Age:**
- Typically age 62 for Social Security purposes
- May vary based on your specific plan

5. NONTAXABLE COMBAT PAY (OPTIONAL)

**For Military Members:**
- Combat pay that's normally not taxed
- You can ELECT to include it as earned income for EITC
- Often beneficial to include it if it increases your EITC
- Make the election on Form 1040

**Example:**
If including combat pay gets you a higher EITC than the tax you'd pay on it, include it.

TYPES OF INCOME THAT DO NOT COUNT AS EARNED INCOME

1. **Social Security Retirement Benefits**
   - Not earned income
   - Doesn't count toward EITC

2. **Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)**
   - Not earned income for EITC purposes
   - Different from long-term disability

3. **Supplemental Security Income (SSI)**
   - Not earned income
   - Government benefit, not work compensation

4. **Unemployment Compensation**
   - Not earned income
   - Payment for being unemployed, not for work

5. **Pensions and Annuities**
   - Not earned income
   - Retirement income, not current work

6. **Alimony and Child Support**
   - Not earned income
   - Not compensation for work

7. **Welfare Benefits**
   - TANF, SNAP, housing assistance
   - Not earned income

8. **Interest and Dividends**
   - Investment income, not earned income
   - Subject to $11,000 investment income limit

9. **Capital Gains**
   - Investment income, not earned income
   - Subject to $11,000 investment income limit

10. **Rental Income (Passive)**
    - Not earned income (unless you're a real estate professional)
    - Investment income

11. **Scholarships and Grants**
    - For tuition and books: not earned income
    - For room and board: taxable but not earned income

12. **Workers' Compensation**
    - Not earned income
    - Payment for injury, not work

13. **Veterans' Benefits**
    - Not earned income
    - Exception: VA work therapy earnings may count

14. **Certain Foreign Earned Income**
    - If you claim foreign earned income exclusion (Form 2555)
    - You cannot claim EITC

SPECIAL CASES AND RULES

CLERGY HOUSING ALLOWANCE:
- Counts as earned income for EITC
- Even though it's not taxable
- Include when calculating earned income

MILITARY BASIC ALLOWANCE FOR HOUSING (BAH):
- Does not count as earned income
- Nontaxable military benefit

DISABILITY INSURANCE PAYMENTS:
- Depends on who paid premiums:
  - **Employer-paid:** May count as earned income before retirement age
  - **You paid:** Not earned income

ENERGY CONSERVATION SUBSIDIES:
- Not earned income
- Utility company payment, not work

ADOPTION ASSISTANCE:
- Not earned income
- Employer benefit, not wages

STATUTORY EMPLOYEES:
- Report income on Schedule C
- Check box 13 on Form W-2
- Counts as earned income

HOW TO CALCULATE YOUR EARNED INCOME

STEP 1: Add all W-2 wages (Box 1)
STEP 2: Add all tips (reported and unreported)
STEP 3: Add net profit from Schedule C
STEP 4: Add net profit from Schedule F
STEP 5: Add union strike benefits
STEP 6: Add long-term disability (if before retirement age)
STEP 7: Add nontaxable combat pay (if you elect to include it)
STEP 8: Subtract 1/2 of self-employment tax

= TOTAL EARNED INCOME FOR EITC

EXAMPLES OF EARNED INCOME CALCULATIONS

EXAMPLE 1: W-2 Employee
- W-2 wages: $28,000
- Tips (unreported): $2,000
- **Total earned income: $30,000**

EXAMPLE 2: Self-Employed
- Gross business income: $50,000
- Business expenses: $18,000
- Net profit: $32,000
- Self-employment tax: $4,521
- Half of SE tax: $2,261
- **Earned income: $29,739** ($32,000 - $2,261)

EXAMPLE 3: Multiple Jobs
- W-2 Job 1: $15,000
- W-2 Job 2: $8,000
- Side business net profit: $5,000
- SE tax on side business: $707
- Half of SE tax: $354
- **Earned income: $27,646** (15,000 + 8,000 + 5,000 - 354)

EXAMPLE 4: Military with Combat Pay
- Regular military pay (taxable): $35,000
- Combat pay (nontaxable): $10,000
- Elect to include combat pay: Yes
- **Earned income: $45,000**

IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

EARNED INCOME vs. ADJUSTED GROSS INCOME (AGI):
- **Earned income:** Only from work
- **AGI:** All income minus certain deductions
- **EITC uses the LESSER of earned income OR AGI**

EARNED INCOME vs. TAXABLE INCOME:
- **Earned income:** Specific types from work
- **Taxable income:** AGI minus standard or itemized deductions
- **EITC based on earned income, not taxable income**

EARNED INCOME vs. GROSS INCOME:
- **Earned income:** Only work-related compensation
- **Gross income:** All income from all sources
- **Earned income is a subset of gross income**

WHERE TO REPORT EARNED INCOME

ON YOUR TAX RETURN:
- Form 1040, line 1: Wages, salaries, tips
- Schedule C: Business income
- Schedule F: Farm income  
- Schedule EIC: If claiming with children

THE IRS WILL VERIFY:
- W-2s and 1099s are matched to your return
- Self-employment income against bank deposits
- All sources of income

WHY IT MATTERS

EITC ELIGIBILITY:
- Must have earned income to qualify
- Amount of credit based on earned income
- Too little earned income = no credit
- Too much earned income = no credit
- There's a "sweet spot" for maximum credit

STRATEGY:
- Understand what counts as earned income
- Report all earned income (IRS will find it anyway)
- Don't confuse earned income with other income types
- Calculate correctly to maximize EITC

COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

❌ Counting Social Security as earned income
❌ Counting unemployment as earned income
❌ Forgetting to subtract 1/2 SE tax
❌ Not reporting cash income
❌ Including investment income as earned income
❌ Confusing earned income with AGI
❌ Not electing to include combat pay when beneficial

✓ Only count income from work
✓ Include ALL W-2 wages
✓ Calculate self-employment income correctly
✓ Report tips (even unreported ones)
✓ Consider combat pay election
✓ Subtract 1/2 of SE tax
✓ Keep good records
