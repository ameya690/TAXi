Common EITC Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Top EITC Errors and Prevention Strategies

MISTAKE #1: Filing Status Errors
Error: Filing as "Married Filing Separately"
- If you're married, you generally cannot claim EITC if you file separately
Exception: You may be able to claim if you meet specific requirements for being "considered unmarried"

How to Avoid:
- If you're married, file jointly to claim EITC
- If separated, check if you meet the requirements to be "considered unmarried"
- Consult IRS Pub 596 Table 2 for your specific situation

MISTAKE #2: Social Security Number Issues
Common Errors:
- Using an ITIN instead of SSN
- SSN not valid for employment
- Expired or missing SSN for qualifying children
- Wrong SSN on tax return

How to Avoid:
- Ensure all SSNs are valid and issued before the tax return due date
- SSNs must be issued by the Social Security Administration (not ITINs)
- Double-check all numbers before filing
- Apply for SSNs well before tax season

MISTAKE #3: Qualifying Child Errors
Common Errors:
- Child doesn't meet all four tests (Relationship, Age, Residency, Joint Return)
- Child didn't live with you for more than 6 months
- Child is over age limits
- Claiming the same child as someone else

How to Avoid:
- Use the IRS EITC Assistant tool online
- Keep detailed records of where your child lived
- Understand the age limits: under 19, or under 24 if full-time student
- Communicate with other family members about who will claim each child
- Use Form 8332 for divorced/separated parents

MISTAKE #4: Income Calculation Errors
Common Errors:
- Including non-earned income as earned income
- Exceeding the investment income limit ($11,000 for 2024)
- Not reporting all income sources
- Incorrectly calculating self-employment income

How to Avoid:
- Only count wages, salaries, tips, and net self-employment income
- Track all investment income separately
- Report ALL income, even if you don't receive a Form 1099 or W-2
- Use Schedule C to properly calculate self-employment income
- Keep excellent business records if self-employed

MISTAKE #5: Ignoring Income Limits
Error: Earning too much to qualify but still claiming EITC
2024 Income Limits (Married Filing Jointly):
- No qualifying children: $24,210
- 1 qualifying child: $55,768
- 2 qualifying children: $62,449
- 3+ qualifying children: $66,819

How to Avoid:
- Check current year income limits before claiming
- Use IRS EITC Assistant to verify eligibility
- Income limits change annually - don't assume you qualify just because you did last year

MISTAKE #6: Not Claiming EITC When Eligible
Why People Miss Out:
- Don't realize they qualify
- Think they don't need to file a return
- Fear of IRS audit
- Complexity of tax rules

How to Fix:
- File a return even if you're not required to
- You can claim EITC for up to 3 prior years by filing amended returns
- Use free tax preparation services (VITA, TCE)
- The IRS wants you to claim credits you're entitled to!

MISTAKE #7: Incorrect Filing Status Claims
Error: Claiming Head of Household incorrectly
To qualify as Head of Household:
- You must be unmarried or considered unmarried
- You paid more than half the cost of keeping up a home
- A qualifying person lived with you for more than half the year

How to Avoid:
- Understand the specific tests for Head of Household
- Keep records of household expenses
- Don't assume you qualify just because you have children

MISTAKE #8: Missing Schedule EIC
Error: Claiming EITC with qualifying children but not attaching Schedule EIC
- If you have qualifying children, you MUST complete and attach Schedule EIC
- Without it, the IRS will reject your EITC claim

How to Avoid:
- Always complete Schedule EIC if you have qualifying children
- Include all required information for each child
- Tax software usually prompts for this automatically

MISTAKE #9: Not Responding to IRS Notices
Error: Ignoring IRS letters about EITC verification
- The IRS may ask you to prove eligibility
- Not responding = denied claim

How to Avoid:
- Always respond to IRS notices within the deadline
- Keep documentation ready: birth certificates, school records, medical records
- Use certified mail when sending documents to the IRS
- Keep copies of everything you send

MISTAKE #10: Fraudulent Claims
Serious Errors:
- Making up qualifying children
- Falsifying income amounts
- Using fake documents
- Claiming children who don't exist

Consequences:
- Banned from claiming EITC for 2-10 years
- Required to pay back ALL incorrectly claimed credits
- Possible criminal prosecution
- Penalties and interest

How to Avoid:
- Only claim what you're legitimately entitled to
- Be honest on your tax return
- Don't let anyone convince you to lie on your return
- Choose your tax preparer carefully - you're responsible for what's on your return

MISTAKE #11: Not Keeping Records
Error: Unable to prove eligibility if audited
Required Records:
- Birth certificates or other proof of relationship
- School records showing dates and address
- Medical records showing address
- Childcare provider records
- Any official document showing child's address and your address match

How to Avoid:
- Keep records for at least 3 years after filing
- Organize documents by tax year
- Make digital copies as backup
- Keep records showing where children lived throughout the year

MISTAKE #12: Relying on Disreputable Tax Preparers
Warning Signs of Bad Preparers:
- Promise large refunds before looking at your records
- Base fees on percentage of refund
- Don't sign returns they prepare
- Don't provide PTIN (Preparer Tax Identification Number)
- Suggest claiming children who don't qualify

How to Avoid:
- Use IRS-certified preparers
- Check preparer credentials
- Use free services if eligible (VITA, TCE)
- Understand your own return before signing
- Report suspected fraud to the IRS

TIPS FOR SUCCESS

1. Use the IRS EITC Assistant tool before filing
2. Keep organized records throughout the year
3. File electronically for faster processing
4. Consider direct deposit for faster refunds
5. Use reputable tax preparation services
6. Review your return carefully before filing
7. Respond promptly to any IRS correspondence
8. Claim prior year credits if you missed them (within 3 years)
9. Stay informed about annual changes to EITC rules
10. When in doubt, consult a qualified tax professional

Remember: The EITC is a valuable credit worth claiming correctly. Taking time to understand the rules and avoid common mistakes ensures you get the credit you deserve without problems later.

Source: IRS Publication 596, IRS EITC Central, Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration Reports
