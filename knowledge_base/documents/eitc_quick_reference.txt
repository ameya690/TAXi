EITC Quick Reference Guide - Fast Facts and Checklists

Quick Eligibility Checklist

DO YOU QUALIFY FOR EITC? Check ALL that apply:

□ I have earned income (wages, salary, self-employment)
□ My investment income is $11,000 or less
□ I have a valid Social Security Number
□ I am a U.S. citizen or resident alien all year
□ I am NOT filing Form 2555 (Foreign Earned Income)
□ My filing status is NOT "Married Filing Separately"
□ I meet the age requirements (if no qualifying children)
□ My income is under the limit for my situation

If ALL boxes are checked, you likely qualify!

• No children: $632

• 3+ children: $7,830

• No children: $18,591

Income Limits (Married Filing Jointly):
• No children: $24,210
• 1 child: $55,768
• 2 children: $62,449
• 3+ children: $66,819

Investment Income Limit: $11,000

The Four Tests for Qualifying Children

A child must pass ALL four tests:

1. RELATIONSHIP
□ Your son, daughter, stepchild, foster child, sibling, or descendant

2. AGE
□ Under 19, OR
□ Under 24 if full-time student, OR
□ Any age if permanently/totally disabled

3. RESIDENCY
□ Lived with you in the U.S. more than 6 months

4. JOINT RETURN
□ Not filing a joint return (or only to claim refund)

Age Requirements (No Qualifying Children)

□ At least age 25 on December 31
□ Under age 65 on December 31
□ Cannot be claimed as a dependent by someone else

What Counts as Earned Income?

YES - These COUNT:
✓ Wages, salaries, tips
✓ Self-employment net income
✓ Union strike benefits
✓ Long-term disability (before retirement age)
✓ Nontaxable combat pay (if you elect to include it)

NO - These DON'T count:
✗ Interest and dividends
✗ Social Security
✗ Unemployment
✗ Pensions
✗ Alimony
✗ Child support
✗ Welfare benefits

Common Reasons for EITC Denial

TOP REASONS EITC IS DENIED:
1. Child doesn't meet all four tests
2. Missing or invalid Social Security Numbers
3. Income too high
4. Investment income over $11,000
5. Filing status "Married Filing Separately"
6. No earned income
7. Missing Schedule EIC (if required)
8. Child claimed by someone else

Red Flags - Don't Do These

🚫 NEVER:
• Claim children who don't qualify
• Make up income amounts
• Use someone else's SSN
• Ignore IRS letters
• Let preparer keep your refund
• File as Head of Household if you don't qualify
• Claim EITC during a ban period

⚠️ WARNING SIGNS of Bad Tax Preparers:
• Promises specific refund before seeing documents
• Charges percentage of refund
• Won't sign your return
• Won't provide their PTIN
• Wants your refund direct deposited to their account

Special Situations Quick Guide

MILITARY:
• Combat pay can be elected to count
• Overseas assignments don't affect residency test

STUDENTS:
• Being a student doesn't disqualify YOU
• Child under 24 can qualify if full-time student

SELF-EMPLOYED:
• Use Schedule C net profit
• Subtract 1/2 of self-employment tax
• Keep good records!

DIVORCED PARENTS:
• Child must live with you 6+ months for EITC
• Form 8332 doesn't affect EITC
• Custodial parent typically claims EITC

FOSTER CHILDREN:
• Must be placed by authorized agency
• Must live with you 6+ months
• Meets relationship test

Prior Year EITC

□ Missed EITC in prior years?
□ Can file amended return (Form 1040-X)
□ Up to 3 years from original due date
□ Can still get your refund!

Example: In 2024, you can still claim:
• 2023 EITC (until April 15, 2027)
• 2022 EITC (until April 15, 2026)
• 2021 EITC (until April 15, 2025)

Calculation Quick Reference

HOW EITC IS CALCULATED:
1. Determine number of qualifying children (0, 1, 2, or 3+)
2. Calculate earned income
3. Calculate AGI
4. Use LOWER of earned income or AGI
5. Find your phase (earning, plateau, or phase-out)
6. Look up credit amount or use IRS tables
7. Verify investment income under $11,000

FORMULA (Simplified):
• Earning phase: Income × Credit %
• Plateau: Maximum credit
• Phase-out: Max credit - (Excess income × Phase-out %)

One-Minute Eligibility Test

Answer these 5 questions:

1. Do you have earned income (from work)? YES/NO
2. Is your income under the limit? YES/NO
3. Do you have a valid SSN? YES/NO
4. Are you a U.S. citizen/resident alien? YES/NO
5. Is your investment income $11,000 or less? YES/NO

If you answered YES to all 5: You likely qualify for EITC!
Use IRS EITC Assistant to verify and estimate your credit amount.

What to Do If...

IF YOU GET AN IRS LETTER:
1. Don't panic - read it carefully
2. Note the deadline
3. Gather requested documents
4. Respond by deadline
5. Keep copies of everything
6. Send certified mail

IF YOUR EITC IS DENIED:
1. Read the explanation
2. File Form 8862 if required
3. Correct any errors
4. Provide additional documentation
5. Appeal if you disagree

IF YOU NEED TO AMEND:
1. Use Form 1040-X
2. Explain the changes
3. Attach supporting documents
4. Allow 16-20 weeks for processing

IF YOU CAN'T PAY:
1. File your return on time anyway
2. Request payment plan
3. Contact IRS to discuss options
4. Don't ignore the issue
