EITC Income Calculation and Earned Income Types

Understanding Earned Income for EITC

Earned income includes all income from employment and self-employment. The EITC is based on your earned income and adjusted gross income (AGI), and you must use the smaller of the two amounts to calculate your credit.

Types of Earned Income:
1. Wages, salaries, and tips - All amounts shown in box 1 of your Form W-2
2. Self-employment income - Net earnings from self-employment (Schedule C or Schedule F)
3. Union strike benefits
4. Long-term disability benefits received before minimum retirement age
5. Nontaxable combat pay (optional - you can elect to include it)

Income That Does NOT Count as Earned Income:
- Interest and dividends
- Pensions and annuities
- Social Security benefits
- Unemployment benefits
- Alimony and child support
- Welfare benefits
- Workers' compensation
- Income of a nonresident alien
- Income earned while an inmate in a penal institution

Investment Income Limit:
For tax year 2024, your investment income must be $11,000 or less to qualify for EITC. Investment income includes:
- Taxable interest
- Tax-exempt interest
- Dividends
- Capital gain net income
- Royalties
- Rental income (passive)

Self-Employment Income Calculation:
If you're self-employed, your earned income is your net earnings from self-employment. This is calculated as:
- Gross income from self-employment
- Minus: Business expenses
- Minus: One-half of self-employment tax
= Net earnings from self-employment

Important Notes:
1. If you have a loss from self-employment, it can reduce your total earned income
2. You cannot have negative earned income for EITC purposes
3. Ministers and church employees may have special rules
4. Conservation Reserve Program (CRP) payments may count as earned income if you elect to treat them as such

AGI vs. Earned Income:
The EITC calculation uses the LESSER of:
- Your adjusted gross income (AGI), OR
- Your earned income

This means if your AGI is higher than your earned income (perhaps due to other income sources), you'll use your earned income for the EITC calculation.

Example Calculation:
John has:
- W-2 wages: $25,000
- Self-employment income: $8,000
- Interest income: $500
- Unemployment: $2,000

His earned income = $25,000 + $8,000 = $33,000
His AGI = $25,000 + $8,000 + $500 + $2,000 = $35,500
His investment income = $500 (under the limit)

For EITC purposes, he uses $33,000 (the lesser amount).

Source: IRS Publication 596, Instructions for Schedule EIC
