IRS Official Guide: Who Qualifies for the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)
Source: IRS.gov Official Website

BASIC QUALIFYING RULES

To qualify for the EITC, you must meet ALL of these requirements:

1. Have EARNED INCOME
Earned income includes wages, salaries, tips, and net self-employment income. See IRS guidance on earned income for complete details.

2. Have INVESTMENT INCOME below the limit
Your investment income must be within the annual limit (check current year limit). Investment income includes interest, dividends, and capital gains.

3. Have a VALID SOCIAL SECURITY NUMBER by the due date of your return (including extensions)
The SSN must be valid for employment purposes and issued on or before the tax return due date.

4. Be a U.S. CITIZEN or RESIDENT ALIEN all year
You must maintain this status for the entire tax year.

5. NOT file Form 2555, Foreign Earned Income
If you claim the foreign earned income exclusion, you cannot claim EITC.

6. Meet certain rules if you are separated from your spouse and not filing a joint tax return
Special rules apply for separated spouses.

SPECIAL QUALIFYING RULES

The EITC has special qualifying rules for:
- Military members (including combat pay elections)
- Clergy members (including housing allowances)
- Taxpayers and their relatives with disabilities

If you're unsure if you qualify for the EITC, use the IRS EITC Assistant tool available on IRS.gov.

VALID SOCIAL SECURITY NUMBER REQUIREMENTS

To qualify for the EITC, you, your spouse if filing jointly, and any child claimed must have a valid Social Security number (SSN) issued on or before the due date of the tax return (including extensions).

AN SSN IS NOT VALID FOR THE EITC IF:
- It was issued after the due date of your return (including extensions), OR
- It was issued solely to apply for or receive a federally funded benefit (such as Medicaid) and does not authorize you to work

IMPORTANT NOTE ABOUT EMPLOYMENT AUTHORIZATION:
If you, your spouse, or your child has a Social Security card with "Not valid for employment" printed on it and the immigration status has changed to a U.S. citizen or permanent resident, you must ask the Social Security Administration for a Social Security card without the legend.

Individual Taxpayer Identification Numbers (ITINs) do NOT qualify for EITC. You must have a valid SSN.

U.S. CITIZEN OR RESIDENT ALIEN REQUIREMENT

To claim the EITC, you must be a U.S. citizen or resident alien for the entire year.

EXCEPTION FOR NONRESIDENT ALIENS:
If you were a nonresident alien for any part of the tax year, you can only claim the EITC if:
- Your filing status is married filing jointly, AND
- Your spouse is a U.S. citizen or resident alien, AND
- You choose to be treated as a U.S. resident

FILING STATUS REQUIREMENTS

Eligible filing statuses to claim EITC:
✓ Married filing jointly
✓ Head of household
✓ Qualifying surviving spouse
✓ Single
✓ Married filing separately (with special rules)

MARRIED FILING SEPARATELY:
Generally, you cannot claim EITC if you file married filing separately. However, there are limited exceptions for taxpayers who are separated from their spouses.

HEAD OF HOUSEHOLD:
To file as head of household, you must:
- Be unmarried or considered unmarried on the last day of the year
- Pay more than half the cost of keeping up a home for the year
- Have a qualifying person live with you for more than half the year

QUALIFYING SURVIVING SPOUSE:
You may qualify as a qualifying surviving spouse if:
- Your spouse died less than 2 years before the tax year you're claiming the EITC
- You did not remarry before the end of that year
- You paid more than half the cost of keeping up a home for the year
- You have a child or stepchild you can claim as a relative (this does not include a foster child) and who lived with you the entire year

For more information on filing status, see IRS Publication 501, Dependents, Standard Deductions, and Filing Information, or use the Interactive Tax Assistant on IRS.gov to determine your filing status.

CLAIMING THE EITC WITHOUT A QUALIFYING CHILD

You can claim the EITC without a qualifying child if you meet the EITC basic qualifying rules AND you:

1. Lived in the UNITED STATES for more than half the tax year
   The United States includes:
   - The 50 states
   - The District of Columbia
   - U.S. military bases
   
   The United States does NOT include:
   - U.S. territories such as Guam, the Virgin Islands or Puerto Rico

2. CANNOT be claimed as a qualifying child of another taxpayer or dependent of another person
   - If someone else can claim you as a qualifying child, you cannot claim EITC
   - If someone else claims you as a dependent, you cannot claim EITC

3. Meet the AGE REQUIREMENTS
   - You must be at least age 25 but under age 65 at the end of the year
   - If married and filing a joint return, at least one spouse must meet the age rule
   - Age is determined as of December 31 of the tax year

SUMMARY OF AGE RULES FOR WORKERS WITHOUT QUALIFYING CHILDREN:
Minimum Age: 25 (on December 31)
Maximum Age: 64 (on December 31)
If married filing jointly: At least ONE spouse must meet the age requirement

OTHER TAX CREDITS YOU MAY QUALIFY FOR

If you qualify for the EITC, you may also qualify for these other tax credits:

1. CHILD TAX CREDIT and CREDIT FOR OTHER DEPENDENTS
   - Provides credit for qualifying children and other dependents
   - Different rules than EITC

2. CHILD AND DEPENDENT CARE CREDIT
   - For expenses paid for care of qualifying persons
   - Enables you to work or look for work

3. EDUCATION CREDITS
   - American Opportunity Tax Credit
   - Lifetime Learning Credit
   - For qualified education expenses

Each credit has its own eligibility rules. Use IRS tools and publications to determine if you qualify for multiple credits.

KEY REMINDERS

✓ You must file a tax return to claim the EITC, even if you don't owe any tax
✓ The EITC is refundable - you can get money back even if you owe no tax
✓ Use IRS Free File if your income qualifies
✓ VITA (Volunteer Income Tax Assistance) provides free tax help
✓ The IRS EITC Assistant can help you determine eligibility
✓ Keep good records in case of an audit
✓ Refunds may be delayed - by law, the IRS cannot issue EITC refunds before mid-February

COMMON DISQUALIFICATIONS

You CANNOT claim EITC if:
✗ You don't have earned income
✗ Your investment income exceeds the annual limit
✗ You file Form 2555 (Foreign Earned Income)
✗ You don't have a valid SSN for everyone on the return
✗ You file as Married Filing Separately (except in limited circumstances)
✗ You are a nonresident alien (unless meeting the exception)
✗ You don't meet the age requirements (if claiming without a child)
✗ Someone else can claim you as a dependent (if claiming without a child)
