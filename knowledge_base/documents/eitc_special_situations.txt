EITC Special Situations and Circumstances

Military Personnel and EITC

Nontaxable Combat Pay:
- Military personnel can elect to include nontaxable combat pay as earned income
- This election can increase your EITC
- Combat pay is shown in box 12 of Form W-2 with code Q
- You choose whether to include it - calculate both ways to see which is better

Benefits of Including Combat Pay:
- May increase your EITC
- May help you meet the earned income requirements
- Can make the difference between qualifying or not qualifying

How to Report:
- Enter the amount from box 12 (code Q) on the EITC Worksheet
- Don't include it elsewhere on your tax return (it's nontaxable)
- Make the election on your return

Military Assignments Overseas:
- Time stationed outside the U.S. still counts as living in the U.S. for EITC purposes
- Your children can meet the residency test even if stationed abroad
- Keep records of your assignment orders

Disability and EITC

Receiving Disability Payments:
Long-term disability benefits received before minimum retirement age COUNT as earned income for EITC if:
- You haven't reached minimum retirement age (defined by your employer's plan)
- The payments are from an employer-paid insurance policy

Disability benefits that DON'T count as earned income:
- Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)
- Supplemental Security Income (SSI)
- VA disability benefits
- Workers' compensation

Disabled Children:
- A permanently and totally disabled child of ANY age can be your qualifying child
- "Permanently and totally disabled" means:
  - Unable to engage in any substantial gainful activity
  - Condition expected to last continuously for at least 12 months or result in death
- You must be able to provide a doctor's certification if requested

Self-Employment and EITC

Calculating Self-Employment Earned Income:
1. Start with net profit from Schedule C or Schedule F
2. Subtract one-half of self-employment tax
3. Result is your earned income from self-employment

Special Self-Employment Situations:

Ministers and Religious Workers:
- Income reported on Form W-2 (box 1) counts as earned income
- Housing allowance is NOT earned income for EITC
- Self-employment earnings from ministerial services count

Statutory Employees:
- Check box 13 on Form W-2
- Report income on Schedule C
- Counts as self-employment income

Farm Income:
- Use Schedule F
- Net farm profit or loss affects earned income
- Conservation Reserve Program (CRP) payments can be elected to count as earned income

Business Losses:
- If you have a loss, it reduces your total earned income
- You cannot have negative earned income
- If loss exceeds other earned income, earned income is zero

Students and EITC

Full-Time Students:
- If YOU are a full-time student, you can still claim EITC if you otherwise qualify
- If your CHILD is a full-time student, they may qualify until age 24
- The student age test is different for taxpayer vs. qualifying child

What Counts as a Student:
- Enrolled for the number of hours/courses considered full-time
- At least 5 months during the year
- At a school with regular teaching staff and curriculum
- Includes colleges, universities, technical schools, trade schools

Work-Study Programs:
- Income from work-study counts as earned income
- Scholarship/grant money generally does NOT count

Separated or Divorced Parents

Who Claims the Child:
- Generally, the custodial parent (who the child lived with for more nights)
- Custodial parent can release the claim using Form 8332
- But Form 8332 only releases dependency exemption and child tax credit, NOT EITC
- For EITC, the child must meet the residency test (lived with you more than 6 months)

Tiebreaker Rules:
If both parents could claim the child:
1. The parent with whom the child lived longer can claim EITC
2. If equal time, the parent with higher AGI can claim EITC
3. If one parent but the other doesn't claim, a non-parent relative with higher AGI might claim

Important: The rules for child tax credit and EITC are different!

Foster Parents and EITC

Foster Children Can Be Qualifying Children If:
- Placed by an authorized placement agency
- Placed by court order
- Child meets all four tests (Relationship, Age, Residency, Joint Return)
- Must have lived with you for more than half the year

Foster Care Payments:
- Difficulty of care payments from state/agency are NOT taxable income
- They also don't count as earned income
- You must have OTHER earned income to claim EITC
- Keep records showing authorized placement

Adoption and EITC

Adopted Children:
- Treated the same as biological children for EITC
- Includes legally adopted children and children placed for adoption
- Must meet all four qualifying child tests

Adoption Assistance Payments:
- Generally not taxable if for qualified adoption expenses
- Don't count as earned income
- You need other earned income to claim EITC

International Adoptions:
- Child must have valid SSN
- Until finalized, may be treated as placed for adoption

Native American/Tribal Issues

Tribal Income:
- Per capita payments from tribal land or natural resources may be excluded from income
- If excluded, they don't count as earned income for EITC
- Check specific tribal agreements and IRS guidance

Reservations:
- Must live in the U.S. for residency test
- Tribal land within the U.S. counts
- Keep documentation of residence

Incarceration

Taxpayers in Prison:
- You cannot claim EITC for any month you were incarcerated
- Even one day in a month makes that month ineligible
- Upon release, you can claim EITC for months not incarcerated if you otherwise qualify

Children of Incarcerated Parents:
- Child can still be your qualifying child if they meet the tests
- Temporary absence (incarceration) counts as living with you if:
  - The absence is temporary
  - You maintain the home
  - Child returns after release

Homeless Individuals and EITC

No Permanent Address:
- You can still claim EITC
- Shelter addresses can be used
- Temporary addresses are acceptable
- Child must have lived with you for more than half the year

Documentation:
- School records showing same address
- Shelter records
- Letters from social service agencies
- Any evidence showing child lived with you

Immigrant and Non-Citizen Issues

Valid Social Security Numbers Required:
- You must have an SSN valid for employment
- Spouse must have an SSN valid for employment (if filing jointly)
- Children must have SSNs valid for employment
- ITINs (Individual Taxpayer Identification Numbers) do NOT qualify for EITC

Resident Aliens:
- Must be U.S. resident alien for entire year
- Use the Substantial Presence Test or Green Card Test
- Nonresident aliens cannot claim EITC

Death of Spouse or Child

Deceased Spouse:
- If spouse died during the year, you may be able to file jointly for that year
- You may qualify as a qualifying surviving spouse in following years
- This affects your income limits and filing status

Deceased Child:
- A child who died during the year can be your qualifying child
- Child must have lived with you for more than half the time they were alive
- You need proof of birth and death

Amending Returns for Missed EITC

You Can Go Back 3 Years:
- If you didn't claim EITC but qualified, file Form 1040-X (Amended Return)
- You have 3 years from the original return due date
- You can still get refunds for those years

How to Amend:
- Complete Form 1040-X
- Attach Schedule EIC if you have qualifying children
- Include any supporting documentation
- Explain why you're amending

COVID-19 Special Rules (If Applicable)

Looking Backward Option:
- For tax years affected by COVID, special rules allowed using prior year income
- Check if any disaster-related special rules apply to your situation
- Economic Impact Payments did NOT count as income

Temporary Work Disruptions:
- Unemployment doesn't count as earned income
- But temporary absence from work didn't affect qualifying child residency test

Remember: Each situation is unique. When in doubt, consult IRS Publication 596 or a qualified tax professional.

Source: IRS Publication 596, IRS Publication 501, IRS Publication 519, Various IRS Notices
