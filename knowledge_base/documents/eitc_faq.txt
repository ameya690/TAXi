Common EITC Questions and Answers

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS ABOUT THE EARNED INCOME TAX CREDIT (EITC)

Q: What is the Earned Income Tax Credit?
A: The EITC is a refundable tax credit for low to moderate-income working individuals and families. It reduces the amount of tax you owe and may give you a refund.

Q: Do I need to have children to claim EITC?
A: No, you can claim EITC without children if you meet income and other requirements. However, the credit amount is smaller without qualifying children.

Q: I'm self-employed. Can I still claim EITC?
A: Yes, self-employed individuals can claim EITC if they meet all eligibility requirements including having earned income from their business.

Q: What counts as earned income for EITC?
A: Earned income includes:
- Wages and salaries
- Tips
- Union strike benefits
- Self-employment income
- Nontaxable combat pay (if elected)
- Disability benefits received before minimum retirement age

Q: What doesn't count as earned income for EITC?
A: These do NOT count as earned income:
- Investment income (interest, dividends)
- Social Security benefits
- Unemployment benefits
- Alimony
- Child support
- Housing allowance for clergy

Q: Can I claim EITC if I'm married but file separately?
A: No, you cannot claim EITC if you file as "Married Filing Separately." You must file jointly or as single/head of household.

Q: I received an advance payment of EITC through my employer. Do I still need to claim it on my tax return?
A: Yes, you still need to file a tax return to claim any additional EITC you may be eligible for, or to reconcile advance payments received.

Q: What if I made a mistake on my return and claimed EITC incorrectly?
A: The IRS may deny your claim and you may have to pay back any credit received plus interest and penalties. It's important to ensure you meet all requirements.

Q: How do I know if my child qualifies for EITC purposes?
A: Your child must be under age 19 (24 if a full-time student), live with you more than half the year, and meet relationship requirements.

Q: I think I'm eligible for EITC but my tax software didn't give me the credit. What should I do?
A: First, double-check that you meet all eligibility requirements. If you believe you're eligible, you can file an amended return (Form 1040-X) or contact a tax professional.

Q: Where can I get more help with EITC questions?
A: Visit IRS.gov/EITC, use the EITC Assistant tool, read Publication 596, or consult a qualified tax professional.

Remember: This is general information. Tax laws change and individual situations vary. Always verify current requirements with official IRS sources.
